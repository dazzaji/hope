## Argument 1 - Written paragraph: check the citations and assertions

Federal copyright law protects original expression but not facts or ideas. *Feist Publications, Inc. v. Rural Telephone Service Co.*, 499 U.S. 340, 345 (1991) ("Originality does not signify novelty."); *Harper & Row, Publishers, Inc. v. Nation Enterprises*, 471 U.S. 539, 547 (1985) ("No author may copyright facts or ideas."). *Feist* also held that effort alone makes factual compilations copyrightable, 499 U.S. at 352 ("'sweat of the brow' remains the touchstone of copyright protection."). And *Baker v. Selden*, 102 U.S. 99, 105 (1879), held that a useful system described in a copyrighted book is itself protected against use by others.

## Argument 2 - Topic sentence only: research the claim, then develop the paragraph

Our client's directory of verified legal-AI vendors qualifies for copyright protection because its selection of vendors and organization into categories reflect original editorial judgment.
